"HUNGER"

3D Narrative Survival Game

Hunger is a first-person narrative survival game about sacrifice, balance, and the psychological cost of enduring. The player must survive ten winter days (December 1- December 10th) while caring for their family and feeding a mysterious creature named Don.

Core Concept

The game takes place on a rural farmhouse during winter. Each day, the player must decide what they are willing to sacrifice in order to survive. Don, a creature living in the barn behind the house, requests abstract needs such as warmth, food, or sharp objects. The player must explore the house and interact with family members to discover items that could satisfy these requests.

Every item given to Don represents a sacrifice. These sacrifices slowly change the state of the player’s home, health, and family relationships.

Core Gameplay Loop

  • Morning: A daily event is given. The player explores the house and talks with family members.
  • Afternoon: Don makes an abstract request (ex: "I need warmth").
  • Evening: The player enters the barn and chooses one item to sacrifice.
  • Night: The world changes based on what was given up.

Player Choices and Sacrifice

Items belong to three hidden categories that track the player's survival state:

  • Home – The condition of the farmhouse and environment.
  • Self – The player's physical health and endurance.
  • Family – The wellbeing and trust of the player's family.

The player can track these categories through an in-game stat bar. They also learn through environmental changes and dialogue.

Examples

  • Sacrificing too many home items causes the house to decay.
  • Sacrificing too many self items makes the player sick or exhausted.
  • Sacrificing too many family items damage relationships.

Environmental Events

Each day, a random event may occur that affects the player’s decisions. These events can create additional challenges or unexpected advantages.

  • Extreme cold temperatures
  • Family Argument
  • Illness affecting the player
  • Family bonding moments that improve relationships

These events modify how many items the player has available to sacrifice, alongside affecting the player's survival categories.

Visual and Gameplay Style

  • First-person perspective
  • Static cinematic cameras inside the house
  • Limited movement between the house and barn
  • Environmental storytelling through world changes

Endings

The player must survive 10 days of brutal winter. The final ending is determined by which survival category was most depleted during the run.

  • Home Ending
  • Self Ending
  • Family Ending
  • Failure Ending (not reaching December 10)

Development Approach

To keep the project achievable within the development timeline, the design focuses on a simplified exploration system, limited locations, and a small set of reusable items and narrative events. This allows the project to focus on atmosphere, storytelling, and meaningful player choices rather than large open-world systems.
